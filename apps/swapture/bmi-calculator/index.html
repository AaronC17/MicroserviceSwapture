What is Body Mass Index (BMI)?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple, internationally recognised measure used to classify adults into weight categories based on their height and weight. Developed in the 19th century by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet, BMI is now widely used by health organisations including the WHO and the CDC as a fast screening tool.

The formula is straightforward: divide your body weight in kilograms by the square of your height in metres (kg/m²). The resulting number places you in one of four categories — Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight, or Obese — each of which correlates with certain health risks.

While BMI is a useful starting point, it has limitations. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat mass, nor does it account for fat distribution. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional for a complete health assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a numerical value calculated from your weight and height. It is a widely used screening tool to categorise individuals as Underweight (<18.5), Normal weight (18.5–24.9), Overweight (25–29.9), or Obese (≥30). It does not directly measure body fat or account for muscle mass, age, or sex.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). In imperial units: BMI = (weight in lbs ÷ height in inches²) × 703. For example, a person weighing 70 kg at 175 cm has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75²) ≈ 22.9 — Normal weight.

What is a healthy BMI range?

According to the WHO, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy for adults. Below 18.5 is Underweight, 25–29.9 is Overweight, and 30 or above is Obese. Thresholds may vary for different ethnic groups and age ranges. Always consult a healthcare professional for personalised advice.
