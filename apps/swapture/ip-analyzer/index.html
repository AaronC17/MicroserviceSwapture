About IP Address Analysis

An IP address (Internet Protocol address) is a unique identifier assigned to every device on the internet or a local network. It allows computers to communicate with each other by routing data to the correct destination. There are two main versions in use today: IPv4 (e.g., 192.168.0.1) and IPv6 (e.g., 2001:0db8::1).

IP geolocation works by mapping an IP address to a physical location using databases maintained by Regional Internet Registries (RIRs) and commercial providers. These databases are updated regularly but may not always reflect the exact location — they typically show the city or region where your ISP routes traffic, which may differ from your physical address.

This tool uses publicly available APIs to provide real-time data including your country, region, city, ISP / organization, timezone, coordinates, and VPN/proxy detection. All lookups are performed client-side directly from your browser — we do not store your IP or any results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an IP address?
An IP (Internet Protocol) address is a unique numerical label assigned to every device connected to a computer network. It serves two main purposes: identifying the host or network interface, and providing the location of the host in the network. IPv4 addresses look like 192.168.1.1, while IPv6 addresses use a longer hexadecimal format.
How can I find my public IP address?
Your public IP address is automatically detected when you visit this page. It is assigned by your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and represents how your device appears to websites on the internet. You can also look up any IP at any time using this free analyzer — no installation required.
What does an IP address reveal about me?
An IP address can reveal your approximate geographic location (country, region, city), your Internet Service Provider (ISP), your timezone, and whether you are using a VPN or proxy service. It does not reveal your exact home address, name, or personal details. Geolocation based on IP is an estimate and may not always be precise.
