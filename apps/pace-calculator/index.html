What Is Running Pace?

Running pace measures how long it takes to cover a unit of distance — typically expressed as minutes per kilometre (min/km) or minutes per mile (min/mi). It is the inverse of speed: a lower pace number means you are running faster. Knowing your pace is essential for pacing a race, planning training runs, and tracking fitness progress over time.

Common Race Pace Targets

A beginner runner typically achieves a pace of 7–9 min/km. Recreational runners often target 5–7 min/km. Competitive club runners aim for 3:30–5:00 min/km, while elite marathon runners hold sub-3:00 min/km for 42.195 km.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my running pace?

Running pace is calculated by dividing your total run time by the distance covered. For example, if you ran 5 km in 30 minutes, your pace is 30 ÷ 5 = 6 minutes per km. Enter your distance and time above and the calculator handles it instantly.

What is the difference between pace and speed?

Pace expresses how long it takes to cover one unit of distance (e.g., 5:30 per km), while speed expresses how much distance is covered per unit of time (e.g., 10.9 km/h). Runners typically track pace; cyclists and vehicles use speed. Both metrics are shown in the results above.

How do I convert pace per km to pace per mile?

Multiply your per-km pace (in seconds) by 1.60934 to get per-mile pace. For example, 5:00/km = 300 × 1.60934 ≈ 483 seconds = 8:03/mile. Our calculator performs this conversion automatically so you always see both values.
