Why password strength matters

Weak passwords are the leading cause of account breaches. Modern hardware can test billions of password combinations per second, making short or predictable passwords trivially easy to crack. A password like password123 can be cracked in milliseconds, while a 16-character random mix of character types could take centuries.

This tool estimates strength using entropy — a measure of how unpredictable your password is. Higher entropy means more possible combinations, making brute-force attacks harder. Use a password manager to generate and store truly random, high-entropy passwords for every account.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a password strong?
A strong password is at least 12 characters long and combines uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. It should avoid common words, predictable patterns like "123456" or "qwerty", and personal information.
How is the crack time estimated?
Crack time is derived from password entropy — log₂(charset_size^length). The number of possible combinations (2^entropy) is divided by an assumed brute-force rate of 10 billion guesses per second to give an estimated time.
Is my password stored or sent anywhere?
No. All analysis runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your password is never transmitted to any server and is not stored or logged anywhere.
